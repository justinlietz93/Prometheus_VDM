# CF17 Plan — Non-Abelian Saturation, Flux Tubes, and Quantitative Confinement in VDM

Date: 2026-03-20  
Status: Planning document  
Purpose: Close the confinement branch quantitatively and nonperturbatively, without smuggling in standard QCD results as assumptions.

---

## 1. Scope

This paper must close the following:

1. why non-abelian color-bearing sectors do not remain as freely propagating isolated charges,
2. how saturation/re-articulation produces boundary-hosted color flux rather than bulk-free charge separation,
3. why flux tube formation is forced,
4. how a quantitative confinement observable emerges,
5. what deconfinement means in VDM terms,
6. what is and is not proven about mass gap, asymptotic freedom, and hadron spectra.

This paper should not attempt to close electroweak mixing or loop/running-coupling physics.

## 3. What must be proven in CF17

## 3.1 Why free color is not an admissible asymptotic state

Need a theorem that isolated non-abelian color charge cannot remain as an unconstrained bulk asymptotic sector.

Required work:

- define color burden in VDM language,
- show why unresolved color-bearing burden cannot terminate on an isolated free source without additional hosting structure,
- connect non-discharge + non-abelian self-coupling + saturation to forced flux continuation.

**Closure target:** a theorem excluding freely isolated color-bearing asymptotic states in the closed non-abelian sector.

## 3.2 Flux-tube formation theorem

Need to prove that color separation concentrates burden into a tube/filament rather than allowing diffuse free radiation to neutralize the system.

Required work:

- define the admissible confined flux-support geometry,
- show why the non-abelian sector minimizes hosting burden by tube concentration under separation,
- derive a stable or metastable tube-supporting solution class,
- connect this to saturation at the boundary of the separating charges.

**Closure target:** a theorem that separated non-abelian color sources induce a boundary-hosted flux tube rather than a freely dispersed color field.

## 3.3 Quantitative confinement observable

Need one hard quantitative object.

Possible options:

- string tension,
- area-law Wilson-type observable,
- energy growing with source separation,
- minimal tube-energy functional with stable radius and increasing length cost.

Required work:

- define the observable in VDM-native terms,
- prove why confinement implies its large-separation behavior,
- tie the observable to either a variational minimum or a monotone energetic cost.

**Closure target:** at least one theorem-grade quantitative confinement criterion.

## 3.4 Tube-radius and internal mode structure

Need to close whether the tube has a preferred internal structure.

Required work:

- define the tube profile class,
- derive the relevant boundary conditions,
- derive or numerically witness the preferred radius/mode family if that is part of the confinement mechanism,
- separate theorem burden from CFN witness burden.

**Closure target:** either an analytic preferred-structure theorem or an explicitly delimited theorem + numeric witness package.

## 3.5 Pair creation / string breaking threshold

Need to explain how confinement coexists with string breaking rather than claiming tubes grow forever.

Required work:

- define the threshold at which accumulated tube burden re-articulates into new boundary-hosted matter pairs,
- show why this does not violate confinement,
- distinguish persistent confinement from infinite unbroken tube growth.

**Closure target:** a theorem or corollary stating the condition under which flux-tube growth re-expresses into new paired structure.

## 3.6 Deconfinement condition

Need a clean statement of what deconfinement means.

Required work:

- define the regime in which the confined tube-supporting structure fails,
- relate it to temperature, density, entropy production, or saturation transition in VDM terms,
- show what observable changes when the system enters the deconfined regime.

**Closure target:** a regime theorem or validation gate distinguishing confined from deconfined sectors.

## 4. Suggested theorem spine

1. **Theorem 1 — Non-abelian asymptotic exclusion theorem**  
   Isolated color-bearing asymptotic states are not admissible in the closed non-abelian sector.

2. **Theorem 2 — Flux concentration theorem**  
   Separation of color-bearing sources concentrates non-abelian burden into a tube-supporting structure.

3. **Definition 1 — Confinement observable**  
   Define the quantity that witnesses confinement quantitatively.

4. **Theorem 3 — Quantitative confinement theorem**  
   The confinement observable grows in the required large-separation regime.

5. **Theorem 4 — Tube structure theorem**  
   The confined tube has a preferred internal support profile / radius / mode family.

6. **Corollary 1 — String breaking threshold**  
   Above a threshold burden, re-articulation produces pair creation rather than infinite linear continuation.

7. **Corollary 2 — Deconfinement regime criterion**  
   Specify when the confinement-supporting structure fails.

## 7. Validation logic required in the CF itself

Required analytical checks:

- no contradiction with CF15 SU(N) current structure,
- non-abelian confined solutions respect admitted gauge structure,
- confinement observable is well-defined and finite in the regimes claimed,
- large-separation asymptotics match the theorem statement,
- string-breaking threshold is consistent with non-discharge and re-articulation.

Required numeric witness criteria to define in the CF:

- existence of a tube-supporting profile,
- positive-curvature interior minimum if preferred radius is claimed,
- monotone energy increase with source separation if linear confinement is claimed,
- stable residuals / convergence criteria for the fitted profile family,
- explicit failure signatures for deconfinement.

## 11. No-open-questions completion criterion

CF17 is complete only if the reader can answer, from the paper alone:

1. Why can’t color exist as an isolated free asymptotic charge?  
2. Why does source separation create a tube rather than diffuse release?  
3. What quantitative observable demonstrates confinement?  
4. Why does that observable have the claimed asymptotic behavior?  
5. When does the tube break into new paired structure?  
6. What exactly distinguishes confinement from deconfinement in VDM terms?

If any of those still reduce to “the numerics suggest it,” the paper is not done.
