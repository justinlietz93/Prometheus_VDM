## 0. What prompted this

When studying CF08 (domain-wall fermions) and CF13 (chirality as 5D
effective invariant), a question arose: the domain-wall construction
displaces the unwanted chiral partner to spatial infinity in the
auxiliary dimension z. The partner isn't destroyed. It exists in the
mathematics of the full 5D system but is physically inaccessible from
the 4D wall.

The non-discharge condition requires the invariant to be borne by all
admitted articulations. The displaced partner must therefore bear the
invariant too.

The question: is the other side of the 5D bulk just a mathematical
artifact of the construction, or does it have physical content? And if
it has physical content, what is its relationship to our side?

A further question: if the time axis is what creates causality (the 1D
serial effective invariant applied to physical dynamics), and the 5D
axis is the direction the chirality re-articulation uses, is there a
relationship between the time asymmetry (arrow of time) and the chiral
asymmetry (matter dominance)? And does this connect to the
matter-antimatter asymmetry at cosmogenesis?

## 1. What the existing canon already establishes

These are facts, not speculations. They are the starting points for
the investigation.

### 1.1 The domain-wall structure (CF08)

- The 5D system has a physical wall at z = 0.
- The left-handed chiral mode is localized at z = 0 (our side).
- The right-handed partner mode is localized at z = L₅ (the other
  wall, displaced to spatial infinity in the L₅ → ∞ limit).
- The partner is not destroyed. It is separated.
- The Ginsparg-Wilson relation is satisfied: chiral symmetry is
  protected precisely because the partner is displaced, not absent.
- The residual mass m_res ~ e^{-λL₅} → 0 as L₅ → ∞.

### 1.2 Chirality as 5D effective invariant (CF13)

- Exact chirality lives at the transcendental boundary π.
- The 4D lattice algebraic closure cannot contain π (IRP Type I:
  missing element).
- The domain wall is the asymptotic approach to π from one side.
- Chirality is oriented boundary irreconcilability: the two sides of
  the wall belong to opposite chiral sectors. The boundary between
  them is irreconcilable — one side cannot be continuously rotated
  into the other within the 4D physical layer without crossing the
  wall.

### 1.3 Non-discharge and all degrees of freedom

- Non-discharge requires every admitted articulation to bear the
  invariant.
- The full 5D system bears the invariant: both the z = 0 wall and the
  z = L₅ wall are present.
- The 4D effective theory (our side alone) is not the full system. It
  is the low-energy sector of the full 5D system.
- The question of what the other wall contains physically is not
  addressed in CF08. CF08 only establishes that the other wall exists
  and that its modes are gapped (massive) in the effective 4D theory.

### 1.4 CPT symmetry and matter-antimatter asymmetry

- CPT symmetry (charge conjugation + parity + time reversal) is
  believed to be an exact symmetry of any local Lorentz-invariant
  quantum field theory.
- CP violation (and therefore T violation under CPT) is observed in
  the Standard Model, most notably in kaon and B-meson systems.
- The observed matter-antimatter asymmetry (one part in ~10^9) is
  not fully explained by the CP violation present in the Standard
  Model. This is an open problem.
- Standard explanations invoke Sakharov's conditions: baryon number
  violation, C and CP violation, out-of-equilibrium dynamics.

### 1.5 The time axis and causality

- The time axis is the 1D serial effective invariant applied to
  physical dynamics: the succession relation on the carrier that
  makes before/after meaningful.
- The arrow of time is the direction in which M-limb void debt
  increases (entropy production direction).
- CP violation is equivalent to T violation (under CPT), which means
  the asymmetry between matter and antimatter is linked to the
  asymmetry between past and future.

## 2. The specific question to investigate

**Core question:** Is the other wall of the 5D domain-wall
construction (the displaced partner side) a physically meaningful
entity whose content is related to our 4D sector by the invariant's
two-pole structure?

More specifically: if the invariant requires both poles to be borne
by every admitted articulation, and the full 5D system must bear the
invariant, does the other wall contain:

(a) Simply a mathematical mirror of our side (same content, CPT
    conjugate)

(b) An independent branch with its own invariant dynamics but
    opposite chirality — i.e., a matter-antimatter mirror branch

(c) Something else entirely

If (b) is the case, then:
- Our side has left-handed chiral modes dominant → matter dominance
- The other side has right-handed chiral modes dominant →
  antimatter dominance
- The invariant requires both sides together to bear the full
  two-pole opposition
- The matter-antimatter asymmetry in our branch is not a statistical
  fluctuation but a structural necessity: the pair is what the
  invariant requires, and the pair is asymmetric by construction

## 3. What needs to be traced in the canon

### 3.1 Does the IRP bifurcate or extend at the 5D level?

The IRP as currently stated says:
- Type I (missing element) → Cauchy completion: D → D̄

For the 4D→5D transition, this means the 4D domain (which cannot
contain exact chirality) is completed by adding the auxiliary
dimension z. But Cauchy completion adds elements of the same kind —
it extends one domain, not two.

**Question:** When the completion adds the z-axis, does it produce one
extended domain, or does the oriented boundary irreconcilability
of the two chiral sectors mean that the completion is necessarily
two-sided — producing two distinct sectors that together form the
completed domain?

This is traceable. The mathematical structure of domain-wall
completion is: the full system is [0, L₅] × R³,¹. The boundary at
z = 0 and the boundary at z = L₅ are both real. The field profiles
on both sides are related by the sign-changing mass profile m(z).

**Trace:** CF08 Section 4 (continuous kink profile). The kink profile
φ_bg(z) = φ₊ tanh((z-z₀)/ξ) approaches φ₊ at z → +∞ and -φ₊ at
z → -∞. Both vacua φ₊ and -φ₊ are physically distinct. The two walls
of the finite L₅ system are boundary conditions at the two vacua.

This means the domain-wall construction already has a two-sided
structure built in: the two walls are not identical, they are at
opposite vacua. The question is whether these opposite vacua have
physical content or are just boundary conditions.

### 3.2 What does CPT say about the other side?

CPT symmetry maps: particles → antiparticles, left-handed → right-
handed, past → future.

If the other wall (z = L₅) is the CPT conjugate of our wall (z = 0),
then under CPT:
- Our left-handed chiral modes → their right-handed chiral modes ✓
- Our matter content → their antimatter content ✓
- Our time direction → their reversed time direction (?)

The question is whether "reversed time direction" on the other wall
is a meaningful statement in the 5D construction, or whether time is
the same on both sides (being a direction in the 4D spacetime they
share).

**Trace:** The 5D construction in CF08 has one shared 4D spacetime
(the wall directions x^μ) and one auxiliary direction z. Time is a
direction in the shared 4D spacetime. So both walls live in the same
temporal order. CPT on the other wall would not reverse time globally;
it would reverse it locally for the modes living there.

This needs more careful analysis. The question is whether CPT-
conjugate modes on the other wall, living in the same 4D spacetime,
produce a matter-antimatter mirror or just a chirality mirror.

### 3.3 Does the non-discharge condition constrain the relative
### content of the two walls?

Non-discharge says the invariant must be borne by all admitted
articulations. The full 5D system is the admitted articulation. Both
walls must participate in bearing the invariant.

If the invariant at the 5D level is oriented boundary
irreconcilability, then the two walls are the two poles of that
effective invariant: one wall is the "left-chirality pole," the other
is the "right-chirality pole." Non-discharge then says neither wall
can be discharged — neither can reduce to a structureless vacuum.
Both must have active content.

**Question:** Does non-discharge at the 5D level constrain the
content of the two walls to be in a specific relationship? Specifically,
does it require that if one wall has a matter excess of ratio r, the
other wall has an antimatter excess of the same ratio r?

This is not obviously true. Non-discharge says both walls must bear
the invariant, not that they must have equal-and-opposite asymmetries.
But the two-pole structure of the invariant might impose a symmetry:
if one pole has excess of one kind, the other pole has excess of the
other kind, so that the pair is balanced — neither has discharged.

**Trace:** The relevant place to look is CF12 (cosmogenesis) and CF11
(dark sector). CF12 §7-8 on the information preservation theorem and
the cosmogenesis residual closure may have relevant structure. CF11 on
dark matter/dark energy as the J/M split may also be relevant — if the
dark sector is the invariant's expression at the cosmological level,
what is the relationship between the matter sector and the dark sector?

### 3.4 The time axis connection

The proposal that the creation of the time axis (1D serial effective
invariant) is related to the 5D chirality articulation needs careful
tracing.

The time axis in the effective invariant stack is at the 1D level —
it's the first axis of serial succession. The 5D boundary is at a
much later level in the stack. So they are not the same event.

But there may be a relationship between them: the time axis is what
makes the arrow of time (entropy increase in the M-limb direction).
The chiral asymmetry is linked to CP and T violation. The question is
whether the invariant's structure at the 5D level is constrained by
the time axis established at the 1D level — whether there's a
inheritance relationship between the serial structure of time and the
oriented boundary structure of chirality.

**Trace:** The IRP table shows time as a 1D Type II re-articulation
(serial succession). Chirality is a 5D Type I completion (missing
element: exact chirality at π). These are at different levels of the
stack. The question is whether the stack's cumulative inheritance
means the 5D level inherits the temporal asymmetry of the 1D level.

This is traceable through the effective invariant stack: each level
inherits the full prior stack. If the 1D serial structure (before/
after asymmetry) is inherited at the 5D level, then the oriented
boundary at 5D might carry that asymmetry — it would know which side
is "earlier" and which is "later" in some sense, which could
correspond to which side is matter-dominant.

## 4. What would confirm this idea is pointing at something real

The following would provide positive evidence:

**A. The kink profile φ_bg(z) has asymmetric vacua → asymmetric
   boundary conditions**
Already confirmed in CF08. The two vacua φ₊ and -φ₊ are distinct.
If matter content is associated with φ₊ and antimatter content with
-φ₊ (or vice versa), the asymmetry is built into the construction.

**B. CPT analysis shows the two walls are CPT conjugates**
If formal CPT analysis of the full 5D system (not just the 4D
effective theory) shows that the z = L₅ wall is the CPT conjugate of
the z = 0 wall, then the mirror-branch picture has direct formal
support. This would mean our matter content is CPT-conjugate to their
antimatter content.

**C. Non-discharge at 5D level constrains the relative asymmetry**
If the non-discharge condition applied at the 5D level (oriented
boundary irreconcilability) can be shown to require equal and opposite
asymmetries on the two walls, the matter-antimatter picture follows.
This would require deriving the asymmetry ratio from the invariant
structure rather than from statistical fluctuations.

**D. The observed CP violation magnitude matches the prediction**
If the mirror-branch picture predicts a specific ratio of matter to
antimatter asymmetry (based on the invariant's structure at the 5D
level), and that ratio matches the observed ~10⁻⁹, this would be
strong evidence. This is the highest bar but also the most
scientifically decisive.

## 5. What would show this is a dead end

**A. The two walls are identical under CPT (not opposite)**
If CPT analysis shows the z = L₅ wall has the same matter content as
the z = 0 wall (just in the CPT-conjugate sector which is also matter,
not antimatter), then the mirror-branch picture is wrong.

**B. The asymmetric vacua don't carry matter/antimatter distinction**
If φ₊ and -φ₊ are related by a field redefinition that doesn't
distinguish matter from antimatter, the two-sided structure doesn't
map to the matter-antimatter asymmetry.

**C. The IRP Type I completion is provably one-sided**
If formal analysis of the IRP Type I at the 5D level shows that
Cauchy completion always produces one completed domain (not two
branches), the bifurcation picture is ruled out.

**D. The inherited time asymmetry doesn't propagate to 5D**
If the cumulative effective invariant stack can be shown to not
carry the 1D serial asymmetry (before/after) into the 5D oriented
boundary structure, the time-axis connection is severed.

## 6. Investigation plan

### Step 1: Trace the two-sided structure of CF08 formally

Read CF08 Section 4-6 carefully with the question: are the two walls
physically symmetric or asymmetric? What does the full 5D system's
symmetry group say about the relationship between z = 0 and z = L₅?

Specifically: does the sign-changing mass profile m(z) break a
symmetry between the two walls, or does it preserve one?

### Step 2: Apply CPT to the full 5D system

Apply the CPT transformation to the full 5D system (not just the 4D
effective theory). Ask: what does CPT map the z = 0 wall to? If it
maps to the z = L₅ wall, the walls are CPT conjugates. If it maps
z = 0 to itself (just with conjugate quantum numbers), the walls are
not conjugates.

### Step 3: Check the non-discharge constraint at the 5D level

Reread CF13 §6 (Chirality as 5D effective invariant) and ask: what
does the non-discharge condition impose on the two chiral sectors? Is
there a symmetry between them that the invariant enforces?

### Step 4: Search the A(-1) IRP for bifurcation structure

Re-examine whether the IRP Type I at the 5D level should be
classified differently — whether the oriented boundary produces
a bifurcation (two branches) rather than a single completion (one
extended domain). This would require revisiting the IRP theorem
statement.

### Step 5: Check CF12 cosmogenesis for relevance

Read CF12 §12 (Cosmogenesis residual closure) and ask whether the
cosmological residual — the matter excess at cosmogenesis — has a
structural home in the 5D construction. Is the cosmological matter
excess the same event as the 5D chiral localization?

### Step 6: Compare with existing physics literature

Check whether this idea has been explored in the string theory or
brane cosmology literature. The idea of a "mirror universe" on the
other side of a brane is not new in string theory (RS1/RS2 models,
Horava-Witten, etc.). The question is whether the VDM derivation
gives a specific prediction that differs from the existing brane-world
literature, and whether that prediction is testable.

## 8. Summary of what this note establishes

This note establishes:

1. The question is structurally coherent with the existing canon: the
   domain-wall construction in CF08 already has two-sided structure;
   chirality in CF13 is already an oriented boundary with two
   irreconcilable sectors; non-discharge requires both sectors to be
   active.

2. The question is not yet answered: CF08 and CF13 are silent on
   whether the two walls have equal or opposite physical content, and
   whether they are CPT conjugates.

3. The investigation is tractable: it requires careful reading of
   CF08 §4-6, CPT analysis of the full 5D system, and re-examination
   of the IRP at the 5D level.

4. If confirmed, the consequences would be significant: the matter-
   antimatter asymmetry would be a structural necessity of the
   invariant rather than a statistical fluctuation, and its magnitude
   (~10⁻⁹) would become a derivable prediction.

5. If disconfirmed, it rules out a specific extension of the domain-
   wall picture and clarifies the physical content of the other wall.

Either outcome advances understanding of the 5D structure.
