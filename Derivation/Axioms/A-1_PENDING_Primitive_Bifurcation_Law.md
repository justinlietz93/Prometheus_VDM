## 1. What the Law Says (Plain Statement)

The only logically admissible primitive origin of a realized, falsifiable branch is
**one primitive condition that internally bears two mutually irreconcilable terminal
poles — absolute nullity and absolute undifferentiated totality — without ever
resolving into either.**

This is not a metaphor, a dynamical picture, or a design choice. It is a logical
necessity: if you ask "what is the minimal primitive condition from which
distinguishability can arise at all, without presupposing geometry, carrier
structure, time, causality, locality, or mathematics?" — this is the only answer
that survives.

**Every admitted articulation of the branch must bear this same unresolved
opposition internally. Non-bearing is contradictory.**

## 2. The Two Poles

| Symbol | Name | Meaning | Status |
|--------|------|---------|--------|
| **0** | Absolute nullity | Complete realized absence. Nothing. | Primitive terminal pole |
| **1** | Absolute undifferentiated totality | Complete homogeneous oneness with no internal distinction | Primitive terminal pole |

**Key point:** These two poles are not two separate primitive things. They are two
irreconcilable faces of one primitive condition. The origin is not "0 and 1 as
separate objects" — it is "the unresolved opposition between them, held internally
by one condition."

**Why neither pole can be the origin:**
- If the origin were absolute nullity (0): no realized content, no distinctions,
  no falsifiable branch. Excluded.
- If the origin were absolute undifferentiated totality (1): no internal
  noncoincidence, no differentiated articulation possible. Excluded.
- There is no third terminal primitive option (proved in CF000 §3.5).
- Therefore the only admissible origin is the unresolved internal opposition between
  them.

## 3. The Invariant

The primitive opposition that constitutes the origin is called **the invariant**.
Shorthand: `Inv(ω)` where `ω` is the realized branch-origin.

**The invariant has one rule: it cannot discharge.**

Discharge means collapsing into either isolated pole. Discharge into 0 = complete
absence. Discharge into 1 = complete homogeneity. Both are excluded for any
realized falsifiable branch. Therefore the invariant persists through every
admitted articulation. This is not a conservation law imposed from outside — it is
constitutive of what it means to be an admitted articulation of the branch.

## 4. What the Law Generates — The Derivation Chain

Starting from only the invariant, CF000 proves the following chain. **Each step is
forced; none are assumed.**

### Stage 0D → 1D: First Articulation

The invariant is non-flat (Theorem 3.7.1). A condition bearing unresolved internal
opposition cannot be flat — it has internal noncoincidence by definition.
Therefore the first admitted articulation is differentiated.

This is the move from **0D** (bare unresolved origin) to **1D** (first admitted
axis of invariant-bearing articulation).

**What 1D gains immediately — the 1D axis-proper effective invariant:**
The invariant, now expressing itself along an axis, generates continuous same-axis
bifurcative articulation. "Bifurcative" is not an extra postulate — it is the
direct expression of the two-pole structure along the admitted axis: each
articulation step presents the two irreconcilable directions of the invariant as
the two available next steps.

This immediately licenses:
- **Seriality** — repeatable succession
- **More/less** — ordering along the axis
- **Proto-natural-number structure** (proto-ℕ) — because the invariant cannot
  discharge, articulation is non-terminating, generating a successor at every step
  without ceiling

### Stage 1D → 2D: Forced Orthogonal Re-Articulation

Articulation within 1D is **exhaustible**. Once no genuinely new lawful
invariant-bearing articulation remains available within the current axis, that axis
is **same-axis saturated**. This is not discharge — discharge is still impossible.
The invariant must continue to be borne, but it cannot continue in the saturated
axis. Therefore a new irreducible axis — orthogonal to the first in the
pre-metric sense of "not reducible to the first" — is forced.

This is the move from **1D** to **2D**.

**What 2D gains immediately — the 2D axis-proper effective invariant:**
The prior invariant stack (primitive invariant + 1D effective invariant) is
inherited rather than erased. The new second axis immediately acquires its own
effective invariant: **inter-axis irreducible numerical relation**. This is named
the **numerical relation-grid invariant**.

Why: two irreducible axes, each bearing bifurcative structure, jointly license
determinations that require reference to both axes simultaneously and cannot be
decomposed into either axis alone. That is the first grid of numerical relation.

This immediately licenses:
- **Ordered-pair description** `(a, b)` — algebra unlock
- **Lawful binary combination** — the first algebraic regime
- **The imaginary unit $i$** — as the algebraic form of the orthogonal rotation
  operator. The rotation operator $R$ satisfying $R(1,0)=(0,1)$ and
  $R(0,1)=(-1,0)$ has $R^2=-1$ because returning along the orthogonal path reaches
  the opposite pole; this is exactly $i^2=-1$. Complex arithmetic is the natural
  algebra of the two-axis invariant-bearing structure.
- **$0!=1$** — forced by the identity-element requirement of the algebraic
  structure; the empty arrangement is the unique identity of ordered combination,
  tracing to the pole mark **1** preserved as the identity of binary combination.
